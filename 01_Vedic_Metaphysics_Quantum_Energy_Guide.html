Module I: Acoustic Softening & Shear Modulus Reduction (G_eff)

1.1 Nonlinear Mesoscopic Elastic (NME) Constitutive Foundation

Polymineralic igneous rocks—specifically tholeiitic basalt (ρ_b ≈ 2,850 kg/m³) and coarse-grained granite (ρ_g ≈ 2,650 kg/m³)—consist of a rigid mineral skeleton (plagioclase, clinopyroxene, quartz) permeated by a compliant mesoscopic bond network (grain boundaries, micro-fissures, dislocation tangles). Under an ultrasonic shear strain field γ(t) = γ₀ + ε_ac cos(ωt), the Guyer–Johnson–McCall Preisach–Mayergoyz (PM) constitutive equation takes the form:

τ(γ, γ̇) = G₀ [ γ + β_G γ² + δ_G γ³ − α_G ( ε_ac γ + ½(γ² − ε_ac²) sgn(γ̇) ) ]

Projecting onto the fundamental harmonic over an acoustic period T_ac = 2π/ω and coupling with the Granato–Lücke dislocation breakaway defect (Φ_GL) yields the strain-conditioned backbone stiffness factor S(ε_ac):

S(ε_ac) ≡ G_backbone(ε_ac) / G₀
        = 1 − α_G (ε_ac − ε_th) H(ε_ac − ε_th) − (3/4)|δ_G| ε_ac² − (Ω Λ L_N² / π²) (ε_cr / ε_ac) exp(−ε_cr / ε_ac)
  • G₀ ≈ 30.0 GPa: Unperturbed linear shear modulus of the intact basalt matrix
  • ε_th ∼ 10⁻⁶: Intergranular micro-slip activation threshold strain
  • α_G ∼ 2.5 × 10³: Hysteretic PM-space conditioning parameter
  • δ_G ∼ −4.0 × 10⁶ (< 0): Fourth-order Landau–Murnaghan cubic softening coefficient
  • ε_cr = F_m / (b G₀ L_c): Granato–Lücke dislocation breakaway strain

1.2 Blaha–Langenecker Acoustoplastic Flow & Griffith Tensile Degradation

Under superimposed quasi-static shear stress τ₀ and ultrasonic stress τ_ac = G_eff ε_ac, the cycle-averaged thermally activated plastic strain rate across energy barrier U₀ with activation volume V* is governed by the zeroth-order modified Bessel function I₀(·):

⟨γ̇_p⟩ = γ̇₀ exp(−(U₀ − V* τ₀) / k_B T) · I₀( V* G_eff ε_ac / k_B T )

Δτ_y(ε_ac) ≈ G_eff(ε_ac) ε_ac − (k_B T / 2V*) ln( 2π V* G_eff ε_ac / k_B T )

Module II: Effective Modulus Dispersion & Negative Stiffness (K_eff⁻¹, ρ_eff)

2.1 Monopolar & Dipolar Local Resonance Homogenization in Basalt/Gabbro

Polymineralic basalt and gabbro matrices contain two distinct classes of sub-wavelength internal resonators:

  1. Monopolar Compressibility Resonators (ω_m): Fluid/gas-filled micro-cavities and compliant grain-boundary pockets of filling fraction F_m that undergo radial volumetric pulsation at strain-shifted frequency ω_m(ε_ac) = ω_{m,0} √S(ε_ac).
  2. Dipolar Inertial Resonators (ω_d): High-density clinopyroxene/magnetite phenocrysts embedded in softer plagioclase rims of mass-coupling fraction F_d that oscillate translationally at ω_d(ε_ac) = ω_{d,0} √S(ε_ac).

Applying dynamic effective medium homogenization yields the coupled Lorentzian dispersion laws for the effective inverse bulk modulus K_eff⁻¹(ω, ε_ac) and effective dynamic mass density ρ_eff(ω, ε_ac):

K_eff⁻¹(ω, ε_ac) = K_NME⁻¹(ε_ac) · [ 1 + (F_m ω_m²(ε_ac)) / (ω_m²(ε_ac) − ω² − i Γ_m(ε_ac) ω) ]

ρ_eff(ω, ε_ac)   = ρ₀ · [ 1 + (F_d ω_d²(ε_ac)) / (ω_d²(ε_ac) − ω² − i Γ_d(ε_ac) ω) ]

2.2 Exact Analytic Conditions for Negative Effective Stiffness & Inertia Inversion

Separating K_eff⁻¹(ω, ε_ac) into real and imaginary components:

Re[K_eff⁻¹(ω)] = K_NME⁻¹ · [ 1 + F_m ω_m² (ω_m² − ω²) / ( (ω_m² − ω²)² + Γ_m² ω² ) ]

In the low-damping limit (Γ_m ≪ ω_m), Negative Effective Bulk Stiffness (Re[K_eff(ω)] < 0) and Dynamic Inertia Inversion (Re[ρ_eff(ω)] < 0) occur inside the anomalous dispersion bands:

Negative Bulk Stiffness Band (Re[K_eff] < 0):   ω_m(ε_ac) < ω < ω_m(ε_ac) √(1 + F_m)
Negative Effective Mass Band (Re[ρ_eff] < 0):   ω_d(ε_ac) < ω < ω_d(ε_ac) √(1 + F_d)

3.1 Asymmetric Bubble Collapse Near a Rigid Basalt Boundary (γ_s = δ / R_max ≲ 2.0)

The radial dynamics R(t) of a cavitation bubble in a liquid slurry of density ρ_l, viscosity μ_l, surface tension σ, and vapor pressure P_v driven by hydrostatic pressure P_h and ultrasonic pressure P_ac(t) obey the Rayleigh–Plesset–Keller–Miksis equation:
